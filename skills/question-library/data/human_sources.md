# Human-tradition generic question sets (paraphrased)

All wording is my own paraphrase. (memory) = from my recall, not a fetched page.

## 1. Polya, How to Solve It
Link: https://www.math.ucla.edu/~abrose/proofwriting/Polyas_howto.html (fetched summary; question detail also (memory))
Axis: stage of reasoning (4 stages).
### Understand
- What exactly is being sought? What is given? What is the condition linking them?
- Can the condition be satisfied at all? Is it too weak, too strong, contradictory, or redundant?
- Can you restate it in your own terms, draw it, or name its parts with notation?
- Can the condition be split into separable pieces?
### Plan
- Have you seen this problem, or a near cousin, before? Do you know a related solved one?
- Is there a known result whose unknown resembles yours? Can you reuse its method or its result?
- Would an auxiliary element (a new variable, a helper construct) make the link visible?
- Can you restate the problem differently, or go back to the definitions of its terms?
- If stuck, is there a simpler, more general, more special, or analogous problem you can solve first?
- Can you solve a part, drop part of the condition, or vary the unknown or data to bring them closer?
- Have you used all the data, the whole condition, and every essential notion?
### Carry out
- Is each step correct? Can you see it is correct, and can you prove it is?
### Look back
- Can you check the result? Can you check the argument?
- Can you derive it a different way? Can you see it at a glance?
- Can you use the result or the method on another problem?

## 2. Heuer / Pherson structured analytic techniques (CIA Tradecraft Primer)
Link: https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/Tradecraft%20Primer-apr09.pdf (fetched summary)
Axis: action requested / technique family (diagnostic, contrarian, imaginative).
### Key Assumptions Check
- What premises does this conclusion rest on, including unstated ones?
- Why must each be true; does it hold under all conditions?
- How confident am I, and on what basis?
- Was it once valid but no longer? Would its failure change the conclusion?
- Which assumptions are really key uncertainties to be tracked?
### Quality of Information Check
- What is known, what is merely reported, what is unknown?
- How reliable and how independent are the sources? Is key reporting corroborated?
- Have I dismissed information that new facts now make relevant?
- Could deception or selective availability explain the gaps?
- Which gaps should be sent out to be filled?
### Indicators / Signposts
- If hypothesis X were true, what observable events would appear over time?
- Which indicators are moving, and toward or away from which hypothesis?
- What would "not emerging" look like?
### Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)
- What is the full set of reasonable explanations, including unattractive ones?
- For each item of evidence, is it consistent, inconsistent, or neutral toward each hypothesis?
- Which evidence actually discriminates between hypotheses rather than fitting all?
- Which hypothesis survives with the fewest contradictions (disprove, don't confirm)?
- Would the ranking flip if one key item were wrong?
- What evidence should exist if a hypothesis were true but is missing?
### Devil's Advocacy
- What is the mainline view, and which of its assumptions is weakest?
- What is the strongest case that the opposite is true?
- Which evidence contradicts the consensus and has been underweighted?
### Team A / Team B
- What is the best case for each competing view, built by someone who holds it?
- What does each side say about the other's weakest point, and how does it rebut?
### High-Impact / Low-Probability
- What is the concrete path to the unlikely outcome? What could trigger it?
- What early signs would show that path is open? What could block or redirect it?
### What If? (assume it happened)
- Suppose the unexpected outcome occurred: what chain of events, worked backward, produced it?
- What are the consequences of being wrong, and how can I hedge?
### Premortem (Klein; (memory))
- Assume this failed badly a year from now: what are the most plausible reasons?
- Which reason is each team member privately worried about but has not voiced?
- What changes now would defuse the top reasons?

## 3. Paul & Elder, six types of Socratic questions
Link: https://psychology.illinoisstate.edu/aehouse/421/421%20log%20folder/Socratic%20questioning.html (fetched summary; sample wording (memory))
Axis: stance / aspect of reasoning probed.
### Clarification
- What do you mean by that? Can you say it another way or give an example?
- How does this connect to what we were discussing?
### Probing assumptions
- What is being taken for granted? Could something else be assumed? How would we test the assumption?
- Why are you assuming this rather than that?
### Probing reasons and evidence
- What supports this? What is an example or counterexample?
- Is the evidence adequate, and how do you know? What would change your view?
### Viewpoints and perspectives
- What is another way to see this? Who would disagree and why?
- What are the strengths and weaknesses of this position versus the alternatives?
### Implications and consequences
- If this is true, what follows? What would that affect?
- Are the consequences acceptable? Does it generalize consistently?
### Questions about the question
- Why am I asking this; what is it for? Is it the right question?
- What does this question depend on? What key term needs defining?

## 4. Alison King, generic question stems
Link: https://columbia.edu/cu/weai/exeas/resources/illustrative-question-stems.html (fetched summary)
Axis: thinking skill requested.
- Application: Give a new example of the concept. How could this be used in practice?
- Prediction: What would happen if a key condition changed?
- Analysis/inference: What follows from this? What are its strengths and weaknesses? Why does it happen?
- Analogy: What is this like? What is a metaphor for it?
- Prior knowledge: What do I already know that bears on this? How does it link to earlier material?
- Cause-effect: How does A influence B? What could produce this result?
- Significance: Why does this matter?
- Compare-contrast: How are these alike and different, on a named dimension?
- Rebuttal: What is the best argument against this claim?
- Evaluation/evidence: Which option is best, and why? Do I agree, and what supports it?
- Synthesis: What combination of ideas would solve this?
- Other perspectives: How would someone else see it?

## 5. Socratic questioning in CBT (Padesky guided discovery)
Link: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/clinical-psychology//CORE/CBT_Competences/Specific_Competences/Guided_Discovery.pdf (fetched); four-stage model also (memory) from Padesky's Dialogues for Discovery
Axis: dialogue stage (sequence).
### Stage 1 Informational questions
- What happened, concretely? What went through your mind? What else do you notice?
- What information about this have you not looked at yet?
### Stage 2 Empathic listening / reflection
- Reflect back: so the picture is X, and the part that matters most is Y; is that right?
### Stage 3 Summarize
- Pull together: of everything found, what are the main pieces, including pieces that do not fit the belief?
### Stage 4 Synthesizing / analytic question
- Given those pieces, how does your original conclusion look now?
- What would you tell a friend in this spot? What is a more balanced reading?
### Cross-cutting moves
- What is the evidence for and against? What other explanations fit the same facts?
- What is the worst, best, and most likely outcome? How could we test it cheaply?
- Guidance: stay curious, do not argue, lecture, or cross-examine; let the person reach the conclusion from their own data.

## 6. US Army After Action Review
Link: https://www.adi.org/downloads/After-Action-Review-Guide.pdf (fetched, a derivative guide); Army original (TC 25-20) (memory)
Axis: stage of reasoning (retrospective).
### What was expected
- What was the goal, who was it for, what outputs counted as success, what obstacles were foreseen?
### What actually happened
- What occurred, in order, and why were particular actions taken? Which planned actions were skipped, and why?
### Why the gap / what went well and badly
- Where did result diverge from intent, and what caused it? What most drove success? Which missteps, and their causes?
### Sustain / improve
- With what we knew then, what could we have done better? With what we know now, what would we change?
- What would we tell the next team? Who does which follow-up, by when? How do we share lessons without blame?

## 7. Toyota 5 Whys
Link: https://aleanjourney.com/?p=24535 (fetched); Ohno origin (memory)
Axis: action requested (causal descent).
- Why did the problem occur? Then, why did that cause occur? Repeat until a cause you can act on and prevent recurrence of.
- Is this answer a symptom or a cause? Has it been verified by direct observation rather than assumed?
- Is there more than one branch of cause at this level?
- Does the chain hold when read in reverse (therefore...)? 
- Does the root point to a process or system gap rather than a person?
- Practice rules: five is a guide, go see the actual place, include those closest to the problem, keep it blame-free, challenge assumptions at each link.

## 8. Coaching "powerful questions": GROW and ICF
Link: https://positivepsychology.com/?p=103120 (search hit, not fetched); content (memory)
Axis: stage of reasoning plus stance (open, non-leading).
### Goal
- What do you want out of this? What would success look like, concretely and measurably?
- Why does it matter, and by when?
### Reality
- Where are things now? What have you tried? What worked, what did not?
- What is in the way? What is in your control versus not?
### Options
- What could you do? What else? If constraints vanished, what then?
- What are the pros and cons of each? Which would you pick and why?
### Will / Way forward
- What exactly will you do, and by when? What could stop you? What support is needed?
- How committed are you, on a scale, and what would raise it?
### ICF-style qualities (memory)
- Ask open questions that evoke discovery; avoid leading or closed ones.
- Ask what the person is learning; ask what is not being said; invite a new perspective; follow silence with patience.

## 9. de Bono Six Thinking Hats (stance taxonomy) (memory)
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Thinking_Hats (not fetched; allowlist)
Axis: stance.
- White (facts): What do we know, what do we need, how will we get it? Which are facts and which are beliefs?
- Red (feelings): What is the gut reaction? What feels off, without having to justify it?
- Black (caution): What could go wrong? Where is it weak, risky, or contradicting evidence?
- Yellow (benefit): What is the value? Why might this work? What is the best realistic outcome?
- Green (creativity): What else could be done? What new angle, provocation, or alternative exists?
- Blue (process): What are we trying to do now? Which stance is needed next? Summarize and decide next step.

## 10. Bloom's taxonomy question stems (memory)
Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_taxonomy (not fetched)
Axis: cognitive level requested (revised: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create).
- Remember: What are the key facts or terms? Which one is it?
- Understand: Explain it in other words. What is the main idea? Give an example.
- Apply: How would this work in a new case? What changes if the situation differs?
- Analyze: What are the parts? How do they relate? What is the underlying motive or structure? What is fact versus inference?
- Evaluate: How good is it by named criteria? What would you choose and why? What is the strongest objection?
- Create: What could be designed or composed that does not exist? What is a different plan?

## 11. Feynman technique (memory)
Link: none canonical (popular attribution; the method is a study practice)
Axis: action requested (explain-to-test).
- Can I explain this in plain words to a novice without jargon?
- Where did my explanation stall, hand-wave, or fall back on a label?
- What did I skip because I only know the name of it, not the mechanism?
- After going back to source, can I explain again more simply, using an analogy?

## 12. Debugging checklists (memory; Agans "Debugging" rules, Zeller scientific method, Weinberg)
Link: not fetched
Axis: action requested / stage.
- What exactly is the symptom? Can I reproduce it reliably, and can I make it fail faster?
- What changed since it last worked? Does it fail in a minimal case?
- What do I actually observe versus what I assume the system is doing?
- What hypotheses fit all the observations? Which cheap experiment would split them?
- Am I changing one thing at a time? Do I have a control (a working case)?
- Am I looking where the problem is or where the light is? Is the tool itself lying?
- Did the fix cause the symptom to vanish, and can I show why? Does the same defect lurk elsewhere?
- Would a written test have caught this?

## 13. Red-team / adversarial review question sets (memory; UFMCS Red Team Handbook, intelligence "alternative analysis")
Link: not fetched
Axis: stance (adversary, outsider, skeptic).
- If I wanted this to fail, what would I do? How would an adversary exploit it?
- What are we assuming about the other side that they would not agree with?
- Whose view is missing? What would they call this plan?
- What does this look like from outside our assumptions and mirror-imaging?
- Where is the plan brittle if one thing is delayed, wrong, or hostile?
- What are the unintended second-order effects?

## 14. Other compact sets (memory)
### Reporter's questions / Kipling six
- Who, what, when, where, why, how: which of these is still unanswered?
### Gary Klein data-frame / sensemaking
- What frame am I using? What data does it ignore? What would make me discard it?
### Fermi / estimation
- What is the order of magnitude? What is the quickest bound, upper and lower?
### Munger-style inversion
- What would guarantee failure? Avoid that. What is the opposite of the claim, and is it plausible?
### Chesterton's fence
- Why does this thing exist? What was it protecting against before I remove or change it?
### Kahneman outside view
- How do cases like this usually turn out? What is the base rate, and why would mine differ?
### Rubber-duck
- Walk through the code or argument aloud line by line: what does each line actually do?
